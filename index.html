Basis and limits

Thicknesses of 175 mm (M1, M2) and 225 mm (M3, M4) come from the preliminary wheel-load screening in the workbook. That screening does not link thickness to traffic, and the workbook labels these values preliminary, not a final pervious concrete design.

The 2 MSA cap uses 20 years of growth at 5%, a vehicle damage factor of 1.7 (IRC:37 indicative value) and a distribution factor of 1. The workbook's own screening scope ends at 50 CVPD, so the slider runs past it and a warning appears above 50.

M1 wins on material cost per m³ and on total cost at every road size and under both supply routes. Material is the only mix-specific cost, so the ranking does not depend on length, width or traffic. The 0.8 flexural reduction in the screening and the 5% RMC discount are unverified assumptions in the workbook.

Maintenance is identical for all four mixes in the source life-cycle workbook. It does not rank the mixes; it only adds to each total. Sweeping, washing and repairs scale with road area, and monitoring is a fixed yearly sum.

Labour and equipment follow the workbook schedule of 24 working days per km, so width does not change placement days. Rates are Andhra Pradesh SoR proxies or stated assumptions. Transport, subbase, GST and overhead are excluded. Verify all rates against current quotations before use.
